What it does

Two things at once, which is the point

Mechanical pressure moves fluid. Red-light infrared works through the tissue so that pressure reaches further. Either alone is a decent treatment; together they do something neither manages on its own.

Mechanical rolling

Rhythmic, rotating pressure applied along the direction of lymphatic flow — encouraging drainage and easing puffiness rather than simply kneading muscle.

Red-light infrared

Infrared light works through soft tissue rather than sitting on the skin as heat — you won't feel it as warmth, but it supports circulation and eases tension so the rolling works on tissue that is already responding.

Myofascial release

Sustained pressure over tissue that infrared light has already reached helps loosen tight fascia and supports range of movement — particularly across the back, hips and thighs.

Circulation

Local blood flow rises in response to the rolling and the infrared light, which is the mechanism behind the recovery benefit and the light, flushed feeling afterwards.

Skin appearance

Red light and improved circulation support skin wellness, and reduced fluid retention contributes to a smoother, more toned look across the treated area.

Deep relaxation

The steady rhythm of the rollers is a reliable way to drop the nervous system down a gear. Most people arrive planning to think and end up doing nothing at all.

Calorie support & cellulite

Standing muscle engagement and increased circulation through a session contribute to calorie burn, and regular sessions help reduce the appearance of cellulite as fluid retention and skin tone improve.

Lymphatic drainage

Why direction matters

Your lymphatic system has no pump of its own. It relies on muscle movement and external pressure to shift fluid — and it only moves one way, toward the node clusters at the neck, armpits and groin.

A roller that works along those routes supports that movement. One that works against them is just a massage. The pattern and direction of the rollers is the part that makes this a drainage treatment rather than a comfortable half hour.

  • Reduced fluid retention — less puffiness and bloating, particularly through the legs and midsection.
  • Waste clearance — the lymphatic system is how the body moves excess fluid and metabolic waste out of tissue.
  • Reduced inflammation — supporting drainage helps settle the swelling that follows hard training or long periods sitting still.
Infrared

Light that goes past the surface

A hot towel warms your skin. Red-light infrared works differently — it doesn't produce that same heat sensation, but it reaches the tissue underneath, which is where the lymphatic vessels and the muscle actually are.

That is the difference between a treatment that feels intense and one that works quietly underneath. Circulation lifts, muscle releases more easily, and the mechanical rolling does its work on tissue that is already responding.

Comfortable, not warm

Most people are surprised there's no heat sensation at all — the effect comes from light and pressure, not warmth. If the rolling ever feels too firm, say so; it is adjustable throughout the session.

Your session

Standing, your choice of length

There is very little to prepare for. This is the easiest treatment in the studio to book and the easiest to turn up to.

Before

Wear comfortable clothing

Soft, close-fitting clothing works best — leggings and a t-shirt rather than jeans. Nothing to remove, no oils, no shower afterwards.

During

Stand and let it work

You stand within the Roll Shaper while the rollers work along the body and red-light infrared passes through. A padded stool is there if you'd like to sit down partway through. Choose a 30, 45 or 60 minute session — position is adjusted to cover different areas.

After

Light, and straight back out

No downtime and no recovery. A light, relaxed feeling is normal. Drink water afterwards — you have just encouraged a lot of fluid to move.

Over time

It builds

One session feels good. Regular sessions are where the drainage, circulation, calorie-burning and cellulite benefits accumulate — most clients settle into weekly or fortnightly.

Suitability

Straightforward, with a few exceptions

This is the gentlest treatment we offer, but infrared light and pressure still rule some people out.

Tell us before you book if any of these apply

Pregnancy · active infection or fever · deep vein thrombosis or a history of blood clots · lymphoedema under medical management · heart or circulatory conditions · uncontrolled high or low blood pressure · recent surgery · open wounds, rashes or skin infection in the treatment area · impaired sensation or neuropathy · implanted electronic devices such as pacemakers · active cancer or current treatment.

None of these is automatically a no, but all of them need a conversation first. We complete a health screen before your first session.
