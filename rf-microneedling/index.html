RF Microneedling

Micro-injury, then heat, then repair

Fine insulated needles create controlled micro-injuries at an adjustable depth. Radiofrequency energy pulses through the exposed tip only — heating the tissue that needs it while the surface is largely spared. The result is new collagen and elastin, produced more aggressively than either technique manages alone.

The basics

Two mechanisms, one wound-healing response

A handheld device presses a grid of fine needles into the skin, then pulses radiofrequency energy through the needle tips once they reach the target depth. The combination triggers the body's natural repair process more aggressively than microneedling or RF manage on their own.

The needle design

Insulated except the very tip

The feature that separates RF microneedling from older energy-based treatments is needle insulation. Each needle is insulated along its full length except for the tip — so radiofrequency energy is delivered only at the precise depth selected, not along the way there.

  • Depth-specific heating — energy is released exactly where the tip sits, from 1mm to 4mm.
  • Epidermis largely spared — the outermost layer is not the target, so it takes little thermal damage.
  • Safer across skin tones — because the treatment doesn't rely on light targeting pigment, the pigmentation risk that comes with some lasers and peels is greatly reduced.
Settings

Depth and energy, matched to the area

We scale needle depth and energy together, and the face, neck and body each call for different settings. Nothing is fixed — your practitioner chooses within this range based on what's being treated.

Settings are not one-size-fits-all

The right depth and energy depend on the area, the concern, and your skin. We assess before every course and adjust as your skin responds — a scale, not a fixed protocol.

Your session

What it actually feels like

This is a more involved treatment than a facial, and there is a genuine, short recovery. Here is the honest sequence.

First

Numbing

Topical anaesthetic is applied and left to take effect before anything touches the skin.

Then

Passes over the area

The device is pressed over the treatment area in a grid pattern, sometimes with more than one pass at different settings. Expect pressure and warmth at the deeper settings; most people find it very manageable with numbing in place.

Straight after

Redness and warmth

The area looks flushed, similar to mild sunburn, with pinpoint marking from the needles. This is expected and settles over the following days.

Following days

Settling

Redness and mild swelling for one to three days, occasionally longer at deeper settings. Skin can feel tight or dry as it heals. Sun protection matters more than usual during this window.

Safety

When it is not appropriate

We complete a health screen before your first session. Any of the following means we either delay treatment or decline it — and we will tell you which, and why.

  • Pregnancy or breastfeeding
  • Active acne, infection or cold sores in the area
  • Keloid or hypertrophic scarring history
  • Bleeding disorders or blood thinners
  • Pacemakers or other implanted electronic devices
  • Isotretinoin use within the last 6 months
  • Active skin cancer or suspicious lesions in the area
  • Recent sunburn or an active spray tan

Before you come in

Come with clean skin, free of makeup where possible. If you're prone to cold sores, tell us — the treatment can trigger an outbreak and a preventative plan may be worth discussing with your GP first.
