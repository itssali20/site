Therapeutic results

What clients come back for

The rapid cooling and precision-controlled pressure of a localised cryotherapy treatment trigger a cascade of physiological responses. In practice, this is what that adds up to.

  • Pain relief — raised pain threshold through reduced nerve conduction velocity.
  • Reduced inflammation — a shift in the balance of inflammatory markers.
  • Accelerated recovery — more oxygen and nutrients delivered to the tissue that needs them.
  • Increased mobility & flexibility — contraction and release through the myorelaxant response.
  • Improved blood flow — around a fourfold increase, sustained for four to six hours.
The science

The five mechanisms, in detail

Most clinics stop at “cold is good for inflammation”. Here is the actual physiology, because it is what separates a targeted CO₂ system from an ice pack.

01

The analgesic effect

Nerve conduction velocity decreases, raising the pain threshold and easing discomfort. Cold exposure also reduces activity in pain fibres directly.

Alongside this, the release of neurotransmitters involved in pain relief and mood regulation — endorphins, serotonin and dopamine — increases.

02

The anti-inflammatory effect

Research indicates extreme cold reduces production of pro-inflammatory markers including TNF-α, IL-1β and IL-6, while increasing anti-inflammatories such as IL-10 and adiponectin.

Combined with the vasoconstriction response, this supports reduced swelling, less chronic inflammation, and limits excess tissue damage.

03

The vasomotor effect — thermic shock

Blood vessels constrict immediately in response to extreme cold. Blood flow to the cooled tissue is momentarily restricted, relieving pressure and diminishing swelling.

As tissue rewarms, vasodilation follows and blood flow increases around fourfold — delivering a far higher volume of oxygen and nutrients. That elevated circulation lasts four to six hours after the session ends.

04

The myorelaxant effect

Extreme cold triggers involuntary muscle contraction followed by relaxation, which supports improved range of motion.

High pressure stimulates Pacinian corpuscles — mechanoreceptors that influence muscle tone and flexibility. This is the component a chilled-air system physically cannot deliver.

05

Enhanced lymphatic drainage

Extreme cold combined with high pressure causes muscles in and near lymph vessels to contract, stimulating the lymphatic system — the body’s natural clearance network. For recovery this means more efficient removal of excess fluid and waste products, reduced swelling and inflammation, improved immunity, and better circulation to support the repair process.

Safety & precision

Guided by thermal imaging

Guesswork is the weak point in most recovery treatment. A thermal camera removes it — before, and after.

01

Pinpoint highly inflamed areas

The camera shows where tissue is running hottest, which is not always where it hurts most.

02

Is it right for you?

Most healthy adults are suitable. Pregnancy and breastfeeding, cold sensitivities such as cold urticaria, impaired peripheral circulation or sensation, neuropathic conditions, open or infected wounds, bleeding disorders or blood thinners, recent surgery, and implanted devices such as pacemakers can all make a treatment unsuitable. We run a health screen before your first session and will say so plainly if cryotherapy is not the right option for you.
