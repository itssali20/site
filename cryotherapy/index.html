The basics

What localised cryotherapy actually is

The controlled exposure of one specific part of the body to extreme cold and high pressure — stimulating a set of natural physiological responses that support recovery and skin health.

The device

INSTANTCRYO™

A steady stream of liquid CO₂ released at −78°C and 52 bar. The combination of extreme cold and high pressure brings skin surface temperature down to 4°C within 30 seconds — and the pressure does work that cold alone cannot.

  • Laser-precision targeting — the treatment area is defined, not approximated.
  • Cold plus mechanical stimulation — 756 PSI stimulates Pacinian corpuscles, influencing muscle tone and pumping the lymphatic system.
  • Zero burn risk — the device carries safety technology that prevents over-cooling of tissue.
  • Lightweight aluminium body — steady, controlled application for the full treatment.

The science

Five responses, triggered by cold and pressure

Rapid cooling combined with precision-controlled pressure sets off a cascade of physiological responses. Here is what is understood to happen beneath the skin.

01

The analgesic effect

Nerve conduction velocity decreases, which raises the pain threshold and eases discomfort.

The release of neurotransmitters involved in pain relief and mood regulation — endorphins, serotonin and dopamine — increases.

02

The anti-inflammatory effect

Research indicates extreme cold exposure reduces the production of pro-inflammatory markers such as TNF-α, IL-1β and IL-6, while increasing anti-inflammatories including IL-10 and adiponectin.

This supports a reduction in chronic inflammation and helps limit excess tissue damage.

03

The vasomotor effect

Blood vessels constrict immediately in response to extreme cold, temporarily reducing blood flow and providing pain and swelling relief.

As the tissue rewarms, vasodilation follows. Blood flow to the area increases around fourfold, delivering oxygen and nutrients — and that boost is sustained for four to six hours.

04

The myorelaxant effect

Extreme cold triggers involuntary muscle contraction followed by relaxation, supporting improved range of motion.

High pressure stimulates Pacinian corpuscles, which influence muscle tone and flexibility. This is the part chilled-air systems cannot reproduce.

05

Enhanced lymphatic drainage

Extreme cold combined with high pressure causes muscles in and near lymph vessels to contract, stimulating the lymphatic system — the body’s natural clearance network. The benefits include more efficient removal of excess fluid and waste, reduced inflammation, and improved skin health.

Safety & precision

We look before we treat

A thermal camera shows what a hand cannot. Inflammation runs hot; restricted circulation runs cold. We map both before a single burst of CO₂ is applied — then measure again afterwards.

01

Pinpoint highly inflamed areas

Thermal imaging identifies where tissue is running hottest, so treatment goes exactly where it is needed.

02

Spot the cold spots

Unusually cold regions indicate poor circulation — frequently the thing sitting behind the symptom.

03

Record the change

Temperature is measured before and after each session, so the effect is documented rather than assumed.

Is it right for you?

Most healthy adults are suitable, but not everyone. Pregnancy and breastfeeding, cold sensitivities such as cold urticaria, impaired circulation or sensation, neurological conditions, bleeding disorders, recent surgery and implanted devices can all rule a treatment out. We complete a health screen before your first session and will tell you plainly if a treatment is not appropriate.
