The science

Five responses, triggered by cold and pressure

Extreme cold combined with high pressure sets off a cascade of physiological responses. Here's what's actually happening under the skin.

Analgesic effect

Nerve conduction velocity decreases, raising the pain threshold. Endorphin, serotonin and dopamine release increases.

Anti-inflammatory effect

Cold exposure is associated with reduced pro-inflammatory markers and increased anti-inflammatory activity.

Vasomotor response

Vessels constrict, then dilate on rewarming. Blood flow to the area increases around fourfold, sustained for four to six hours.

Myorelaxant effect

Cold triggers involuntary contraction then relaxation. High pressure stimulates Pacinian corpuscles, influencing muscle tone.

Lymphatic drainage

Muscles near lymph vessels contract, supporting the body's clearance of excess fluid and waste.
